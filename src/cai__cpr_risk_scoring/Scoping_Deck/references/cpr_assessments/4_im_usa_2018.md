# Prior Compliance Process Review Assessment — Findings from Previous CPR (IM USA, 2018)

Overall, 38 observations were identified: 11 critical, 18 major, and 9 minor. No observations were identified as repeat findings.

## Fee for Service
- Major (6): Lack of GO identification; missing documentation for proof of services; missing FCPA clause on contracts.

## Due Diligence (DD)
- Critical (1): Evergreen contracts with sub-intermediaries.
- Major (2): Missing HCBIG clauses on contracts with sub-intermediaries.

## Tenders
- Critical (1): Missing documentation to evidence pre-tender discussions with legal; missing documentation of the business rationale for participating or not participating in tenders.

## Distributor Payments and Margins
- Critical (3): Process to calculate FMV for new sales, renewals, or scope changes for existing TPIs was not in line with the HCC Distributor Compensation Playbook; lack of an SOP for price and margins per franchise/customer.

## Educational Grants
- Critical (3): Funds with an educational purpose were given directly to HCPs rather than the institutions hosting the event; lack of a grant committee inclusive of the HCCO.
- Major (1): Lack of a reconciliation process over grant funds.

## Congresses
- Critical (1): Lack of HCCO pre-approvals of GO financial interactions.
- Major (2): Missing documentation for proof of services.

## Expense Reports
- Major (4): Inadequate supporting documentation for expenses; missing documentation for business purpose, attendance sheet or meal receipts; review process control for approving expense reports not followed.

## Sponsorships
- Major (2): Company did not retain sufficient documentation to evidence the activity took place.

## Product & Procedure Training
- Critical (1): Lack of HCCO pre-approvals of GO financial interactions.
- Major (1): System programming error caused forms uploaded with HCP selection criteria to appear empty.

## HCC Environment
- Critical (1): Lack of a process to review contract templates for alignment with updated J&J policies, local regulations or other corporate/business changes.
