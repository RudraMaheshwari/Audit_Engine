# MedTech Kuwait — Business Summary

## Business Overview
- MedTech Kuwait Business Units: DePuy Synthes ("DPS"); One Ethicon ("OneEth"); Cardio & Specialty Solutions ("CSS").
- Net Trade Sales (NTS) in 2023 – USD 145 millions (MM) (Ethicon – 82%, DPS – 13% and CSS – 5%).
- NTS year-to-date (November 2024) – USD 98 MM (20% behind the business plan which is USD 121.5MM).

## Changes and Challenges within the Business
- New HCCO for MedTech Kuwait, effective July 2024.
- New Controller for MedTech Kuwait, effective January 2024.
- New Legal Director for MedTech Kuwait, effective January 2024.
- Local legal entity / Regional Headquarters (RHQ) established in Kuwait effective January 1, 2024.
- Procurement through National Unified Procurement Company (NUPCO) for all tenders on national level. Limited procurement through Gulf Health Council (GHC) – centralized for Gulf countries.

## Go-to-Market Approach
- Direct vs Indirect: currently Direct sales 25% and Indirect 75%. Go-to-market model is in transition to Direct which is estimated to be approx. 94% by end of 2025.
- Public vs Private: Public customers – 70%, Private customers – 30%.
- Tender vs Non-Tender: Tender – 80%, Non-tender – 20%.

## Interactions with Public HCPs/GOs/HCIs
- Interaction with HCPs/GOs/HCIs as part of tenders, consultancy arrangements, and medical education/congresses.

## Third Party Intermediary (TPIs) Overview
- There are 10 TPIs, including 8 sales intermediaries (distributors) and 2 non-sales intermediaries (1 warehouse provider and 1 regulatory intermediary).
- Distributors are compensated through margins.
- Farouk Maamoun Tamer and Co provides warehouse management services and is compensated through a fixed fee.
- Mazen Abdullah Hubishi Trading is a regulatory intermediary used by J&J to make payments to the government as J&J does not have access to the system. Mazen is compensated through a percentage of total government fees.

## Business in Current / Following Year
- 2024 was a year of transformation – MedTech Kuwait went through a go-to-market model change from distributor model to direct model since January 2024, which had some impact on 2024 business performance, including stock buyback from distributors, black-out period during contract transfers, and supply chain disruptions.
